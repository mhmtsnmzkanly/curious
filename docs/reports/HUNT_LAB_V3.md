## Interpretation and limits

The preregistered risk policy did not reduce injuries in the defensive track. In the normal 320 evaluation trials, `experienced_risk_online` and its risk-frozen counterpart injured 248 trials and incurred 3,558 prey-defense damage, compared with 215 injured trials and 3,276 damage for `experienced_online`. The risk-aware arms acquired food in 313/320 trials versus 308/320, but spent 7,505 energy versus 6,043 and ended with mean health 119.600 versus 125.150. The same-table risk-off frozen ablation had 214 injured trials, 2,904 prey-defense damage, food in 273/320 trials, 6,820 energy spent, and mean final health 124.466.

After reversal, risk-online injured 246/320 trials and took 1,476 prey-defense damage, versus 218/320 and 1,308 for plain experienced-online; food was acquired in 278 versus 282 trials. These descriptive outcomes do not support a risk-reduction or net-benefit claim. The predeclared mechanism ran and its audit passed, but this particular policy did not improve the measured injury outcomes. The study has only eight paired seed schedules per track, a bounded synthetic setting, and its risk context is coarse; it does not establish real-world animal learning or justify deployment. No post-result tuning or parameter sweep was performed. Keep the feature disabled in live settings pending a separately preregistered design with a justified hypothesis.

The run completed 10,240 trials and 99,729 physical ticks, below the registered 204,800-tick ceiling. The runner validated all six arms, risk-counter continuity, frozen-table equality, and the declared observation rules.
